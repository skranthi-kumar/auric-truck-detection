# Literature notes for the report (11 Oct; from an external AI reviewer who read 5 papers in full, others in excerpts)
In full: Shermeyer & Van Etten (SR on xView), SAHI, DN-TOD, Dataset Cartography, VPS pruning. Partly: CLOD (abstract only), NWD, xView, dense-grid paper.
Use as priors and context only; nothing here changes a declared rule.

## Corrections to earlier framing
- **Dataset Cartography (Swayamdipta et al. 2020):** "33% subset matches full data" holds for the NLI sets. On WinoGrande the 33% ambiguous subset scored 78.7 vs 79.7 (full, in-distribution); ambiguous-only subsets of <= 17% failed to train; adding ~10% easy examples fixed that. Do not cite it as "a third of the data suffices" in general.
- **CLOD's 16-46% mAP gain from label cleaning:** unverified (experiments not read). Measured reference instead: **DN-TOD**, AI-TOD (noisy) vs AI-TOD-v2.0 (relabelled): AP50 32.6 vs 34.9, about +2 points. Label cleaning stays a modest-gain item.

## Where 0.139 sits (protocols differ; context, not a like-for-like comparison)
- **SAHI on xView:** full-image inference ~2 AP50; slicing-aided fine-tuning + sliced inference ~16-20 AP50 over 60 classes. Their error analysis: the main headroom is category confusion and false positives (same as our oracle dAP: cls largest).
- **DN-TOD, AI-TOD-v2.0 (clean labels, mean object 12.7 px):** AP50 34.9 (one-stage), 45.0 (two-stage).
- **YOLT / Shermeyer & Van Etten:** Bus/Truck merged, IoU 0.25, 30 cm: AP 0.38.
- Dense-grid paper (excerpts only): 0.49-0.52 on all 60 xView classes under its own protocol.
- -> 0.139 on five fine-grained truck classes at IoU 0.5 is within the range of the papers reviewed. Wording for the report: "none of the papers reviewed report anything near 0.75 under comparable protocols" (protocols differ: class sets, IoU threshold, splits, GSD). Not a claim about the whole literature.

## Priors for our open items
- **Upsampling:** SAHI fine-tunes on 300-500 px slices resized to 800-1333 px (2-4x), consistent with our 0.073 -> 0.107 (1x -> 2x) and with 3x not adding more at 25% (P3_up3 0.059).
- **5.1c resolution test:** Shermeyer & Van Etten: Bus/Truck AP 0.38 (30 cm) -> 0.32 (60 cm), so a drop at 2x downsampling is the PRIOR. Direction only: that is a detector, merged-class AP at IoU 0.25; ours is a 5-class crop classifier. They note halving image size differs from a coarser sensor (they blur before decimating). Our test (PIL BOX area-averaging, then resize to 128) isolates information content only; it does not simulate sensor optics.
- **Label cleaning (if ever run):** DN-TOD: relabelling by the model's top class pushes labels toward the dominant class (here Cargo/Box; Tractor/Liquid lose). Use class-specific thresholds, drop or down-weight; never argmax-relabel. Missing/extra labels hurt little; class shifts and box offsets hurt a lot. Wording: the 34% Cargo<->Box figure is a model-vs-GT DISAGREEMENT rate, not measured noise; "if it reflects label noise, it is class-shift noise, which DN-TOD found among the most damaging in synthetic experiments". DN-TOD's +2 AP50 is a reference from another dataset, not a prediction for ours.
- **Check-3 audit template (Cartography):** two annotators blind-relabel 50 predicted-noisy + 50 predicted-clean; WinoGrande: 67% vs 13% mislabelled/ambiguous. Needs annotators who can tell truck types apart (our non-expert audit was at chance). They also report seed-to-seed correlation of per-example scores (>= 0.75); we have one seed for cartography, so we cannot report it (state as a limit).
- **5.4 subset selection, by task (cite separately):**
  - Cartography (NLP): easy-only lost to random (WinoGrande 69.4 vs 73.3), matching our result; hard/ambiguous-only beat random on NLI.
  - VPS pruning (detection): hardest-image selection lost to random at high pruning; a variance-based score beat random only modestly; epochs held equal across sizes. Our rule ranked images by share of easy/medium/ambiguous instances (i.e. it excluded hard-confused/hard-missed), stratified by rarest class, instance-matched budget, 30 epochs for every size (epochs equal). It tied random at 12.5% and lost at 25%. Our easy-leaning loss matches Cartography's easy-only result. Cartography's ~10% easy mix fixed NON-CONVERGENCE of tiny ambiguous-only subsets, not losing to random; untested here and relevant only if small subsets underfit.
- **Rough benchmark (reviewer's log-linear extrapolation of our curve, not a bar):** at +0.015 per doubling from 0.0882, a random subset would reach 90% of full (0.079) at about 2/3 of the data, range 37-78% across the slope CI; rests on single-seed 50%/100% points. Our selected subsets did not reach 90% at any tested size (largest 50%).

## Deep research (11 Oct): published scores for the 5 xView truck subtypes (adversarially verified, 3-vote)

I found no published per-class AP50 near 0.75 for any of the five truck subtypes. Every source puts them far below that. In the 2018 baseline paper (Lam et al., SSD), these trucks score between 0.000 and 0.474 AP, and most of them are under 0.13. The 2021 OBB paper (Zand et al., TGRS) reports Cargo Truck at 0.13 and Truck Tractor at 0.24. Across all 60 classes, the best published xView results are only about 0.2-0.3 mAP50: the 2018 winners reached about 29-32, SAHI about 20.6, ASAHI about 25.7 and CO-DETR Swin-L about 19.7. Even a single merged 'vehicle' class tops out at about 0.66-0.68. The biggest documented gains came from two things. The first is slicing or tiling, with slicing-aided fine-tuning and overlapping sliced inference adding roughly +10 to +18 AP50. The second is upsampling at the 0.3 m native resolution: 2x super-resolution gave a 13-36% relative mAP gain, and 4x upscaling plus two-stage tiling raised merged-vehicle AP from 0.45 to 0.68. Plain nearest-neighbour upscaling matched learned super-resolution. So with about 443 images, free T4 compute and 4 days, 0.75 mAP50 on these five classes is very unlikely. The current 0.195 zero-shot score from the xView-trained RF-DETR is already in line with published per-class truck numbers, and the realistic levers are tiling at 2x to 4x upsampling plus fine-tuning that checkpoint.


- **Published per-class AP for the target truck subtypes is very low, in the 0.00-0.47 range, and usually under 0.25. No paper reports anything near 0.75 for these classes.** (high, vote 3-0 (both)). Lam et al. 2018 (SSD, 60-class model), reported as vanilla / multi-resolution / augmented: Cargo Truck 0.049/0.097/0.045; Truck Tractor 0.005/0.213/0.000; Box 0.136/0.086/0.119; Flatbed 0.032/0.126/0.034; Liquid 0.018/0.474/0.000. The mapping from the paper's row names to the released class names is my inference. Zand et al. 2021 (19-class small-object subset, horizontal boxes, IoU threshold not stated): Cargo Truck 0.1345 and Truck Tractor 0.2435 for the best method; the baselines (SSD, YOLOv3, YOLT, RFL) score 0.089-0.115 on Cargo Truck. That test split likely shares tiles from the same scenes into training, so its scores may be inflated. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.07856, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.11854
- **The best results over all 60 xView classes are only about 0.20-0.32 mAP50, whether from the 2018 challenge winners or from modern detectors.** (high, vote 3-0 except 2010.06520 (2-1)). 1st place in 2018 used an FPN Faster R-CNN with a ResNet-50 backbone, Reduced Focal Loss and random skipping of the most frequent classes. A single model scored 28.32 on validation and 26.75 on the public leaderboard. The ensemble with test-time augmentation scored 31.74 public and 29.32 private, against 21.78 for the organisers' SSD. A secondary source cites the challenge best as 29% mAP. Lam et al.'s best baseline reached 0.259. In a 2025 benchmark of 11 detectors, the best was CO-DETR with Swin-L at 19.69 AP50, using plain 512 px tiles. None of these papers report the truck subtypes separately. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.01347, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.06520, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.02871, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.07856
- **Slicing or tiling gives the largest documented gain on xView. The best setup combines slicing-aided fine-tuning, sliced inference and about 25% patch overlap; multi-scale chipping also helps.** (high, vote 3-0 (ASAHI test number 2-1)). SAHI on xView: full-image inference gives about 2.1-2.2 AP50, and slicing-aided fine-tuning plus sliced inference with overlap gives 17.1 / 17.7 / 20.6 (FCOS / VFNet / TOOD). Small-object AP50 is 14.9. Overlap adds about 1-1.7 points. ASAHI with TPH-YOLOv5 on validation goes from 14.8 to 24.4 with slicing, and to 25.7 with the full stack. ASAHI on the test split scores 22.7 vs 20.4 for SAHI, but that paper is a questionable preprint and one of its tables is internally inconsistent. In Lam et al., multi-resolution chips at 300/400/500 px raised mAP from 0.146 to 0.259. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.06934, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19233, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.07856
- **Upsampling at the 0.3 m native resolution (2x-4x) gives large gains, and simple nearest-neighbour upscaling works as well as learned super-resolution.** (high, vote 3-0). Shermeyer & Van Etten (CVPRW 2019): 2x super-resolution from 30 cm to 15 cm gave a 13-36% relative mAP gain; YOLT went from 0.53 to 0.60 and SSD from 0.30 to 0.36-0.41. These are coarse merged classes, and the YOLT gain is only +1.7 sigma. Koester & Sahin 2019 (YOLOv2, one merged vehicle class): 4x upscaling plus two-stage tiling raised AP from 0.4548 to 0.683. Learned super-resolution (MDSR) scored 0.6833 vs 0.6835 for nearest-neighbour, so the gain comes from object size, not from super-resolution quality. A small-venue VapSwinSR paper reports +14.26% mAP50 with YOLOv8, without giving its baseline. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.04098, https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPRW_2019/papers/EarthVision/Shermeyer_The_Effects_of_Super-Resolution_on_Object_Detection_Performance_in_Satellite_CVPRW_2019_paper.pdf, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.05283, https://scholar.its.ac.id/en/publications/hybrid-attention-based-super-resolution-for-satellite-imagery-usi/
- **Even class-agnostic 'vehicle' detection at full 0.3 m resolution stays below 0.75 mAP50. Most super-resolution papers merge all trucks into one class, so they give no evidence about the subtypes.** (medium, vote 3-0 except the YOLOv3 numbers (2-1)). Joint-SRVDNet (YOLOv3, 512 px crops, single 'vehicle' class): 66.02 mAP@0.5 on the original high-resolution images, and 53.5-61.5 on super-resolved images; its mAP definition is non-standard. The 4x NN merged-vehicle result is 0.683. Shermeyer merges all truck subtypes into a Bus/Truck class, citing inconsistent labels, few examples (Truck w/Liquid has 149) and look-alike classes (Box has 3653 examples). Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00983, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.04098, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.05283
- **Telling the subtypes apart is hard in itself and the labels are noisy. Even with ground-truth boxes, xView classification reaches only 73% accuracy.** (medium, vote 2-1 / 3-0). A DenseNet-161 trained on ground-truth crops (61 classes, 1 epoch) reached 73% overall, with Utility Truck at 28%; Utility Truck is not one of the target classes. The authors put part of this down to inconsistent labelling. Shermeyer also notes that Box and Liquid look very similar. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.06520, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.04098
- **Public aerial-pretrained starting points exist: Ultralytics OBB checkpoints pretrained on DOTAv1, and the SatlasPretrain Swin and ResNet backbones. Neither is trained on xView, and neither covers the truck subtypes.** (high, vote 3-0). The OBB models output rotated boxes, so only the backbone would transfer to xView's axis-aligned boxes. SatlasPretrain imagery is 0.5-2 m GSD, coarser than xView's 0.3 m. One claim that SatlasPretrain ships with no detection head was refuted 0-3. The xView-trained RF-DETR checkpoint the user already has is likely the strongest available prior. Sources: https://docs.ultralytics.com/tasks/obb, https://huggingface.co/allenai/satlas-pretrain

Caveats:
- Almost every number is a 60-class or merged-class mAP, so none of them directly measures 5-class truck mAP50. A 5-class in-domain subset could score higher than the 60-class averages.
- The evaluation splits vary (random 75/25, 80/10/10, 80/20) and some likely share tiles between train and test. IoU thresholds and mAP definitions also vary.
- The ASAHI preprint (2604.19233) has an internally inconsistent table and an unusual affiliation.
- The 'ceiling' framing from the 2018 challenge overstates things: 29% was the best score of 2018, not a hard limit.
- Most methods are from 2018-2022. Modern DINOv2-based detectors with an xView-pretrained checkpoint are not benchmarked per-class in the literature.
- The super-resolution gains come from coarse classes, and the VapSwinSR result is from a weak venue with an unknown baseline.
- The mapping from Lam et al.'s row names to the released class names is my inference.

## Deep research 2 (11 Oct): general small-object components for fine-tuning a DETR (verified, 3-vote)

The best-supported general small-object levers are tiny-object-aware box metrics (NWD, RFLA, SimD) and DETR-specific density and query changes (DQ-DETR, Dome-DETR). The metrics give +1.8 to +9.6 AP on AI-TOD, but every number comes from anchor-based CNN detectors. None was tested inside DETR Hungarian matching, so using them in RF-DETR would be our own adaptation. DETR-native methods (DQ-DETR +7.3 AP50 over DINO; Dome-DETR +0.6 AP50 over DQ-DETR; density-adaptive query count +2.0 AP on D-FINE-S) are proven, but they are architecture changes, not cheap fine-tuning add-ons. Augmentation and sampling are the cheapest to plug in, but their measured gains are modest. Copy-paste adds about +1.3 box AP on top of scale jitter when data is plentiful, and +4.8 at 10% of COCO. Naive random pasting slightly hurts on VisDrone (RT-DETR -0.2 AP50). Sampler fixes (RFS/IRFS) help rare classes most. For a 5-class, 443-image, 15-20 px truck detector, the expected gain per hour ranks roughly as follows (our inference): (1) class-aware repeat-factor sampling (IRFS) plus scale jitter, under 1 h. (2) A larger query count / num_select for dense depots (medium cost; supported only indirectly, by Dome-DETR's +2.0 AP from adaptive queries). (3) Context-aware copy-paste that keeps the original images, about 2-4 h, expect +0 to +2. (4) An NWD-style term added to the Hungarian cost and box loss, about 3-6 h; high potential but untested in DETR. (5) Porting DQ-DETR or Dome-DETR modules, multiple days, not realistic in 3 days. Inference levers (slicing with overlap, upscaling, TTA/WBF) and second-stage crop classifiers had no claims survive in this round, so their ranking rests on the earlier use-case research, not on this run.


- **Gaussian or receptive-field box metrics (NWD, RFLA, SimD) give the largest reported tiny-object gains on AI-TOD by fixing IoU-based label assignment. IoU is very sensitive to small location shifts on tiny boxes, so many tiny ground-truth boxes end up with no positive match.** (high, 3-0 (merged claims 0,2,4,5,6,9)). NWD: +6.7 AP over a fine-tuning baseline on AI-TOD (Faster R-CNN). Most of the gain comes from assignment; NWD replaces IoU in assignment, NMS and the loss. NWD-RKA + DetectoRS: +4.3 AP over SOTA on AI-TOD-v2. RFLA: switching from IoU to RFD assignment adds +9.6 AP (11.1 baseline, Faster R-CNN); DetectoRS+RFLA reaches 24.8 AP / 55.2 AP50 (+4.0 over SOTA); one-stage detectors gain only modestly. SimD: +1.8 AP overall and +4.1 APvt over SOTA. All votes 3-0. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13389, https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13996, https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08738, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02394
- **None of these metrics has been validated inside DETR Hungarian matching. Using them in RF-DETR means adding NWD/SimD-style terms to the matching cost and/or regression loss, which is an untested adaptation. The anchor-assignment mechanism behind the large gains does not exist in DETR's one-to-one matching, so the expected gain is much smaller and uncertain.** (high, 3-0 (merged claims 1,3,7,10)). Each paper scopes itself to anchor-based (or dense anchor-free) detectors. The RFLA authors say end-to-end DETR performance on tiny objects 'requires further investigation'. Verifiers found only non-peer-reviewed RT-DETR preprints (PF-DETR, SO-RTDETR) that add an NWD loss, and no work that uses NWD/RFLA/SimD as a Hungarian cost. Cost on top of our loop: about 3-6 h to add an NWD cost/loss term. Risk: it could destabilise matching or have no effect. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13389, https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13996, https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08738, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02394
- **DETR-native small-object methods deliver real gains over DINO on AI-TOD-V2, mostly at strict IoU and on very tiny objects. Measured by AP50, most of the gain comes in the first step from DINO to DQ-DETR.** (medium, 3-0 (merged claims 8,11,13)). DQ-DETR R50: 30.2 AP / 68.6 AP50, against 25.9 / 61.3 for a re-implemented DINO (+4.3 AP, +7.3 AP50). Dome-DETR-L: 34.6 AP / 69.2 AP50 / 19.0 APvt, only +0.6 AP50 over DQ-DETR but +4.4 AP and +9.7 AP75. HEDS-DETR on VisDrone: +3.8 AP / +5.1 AP50 over an unnamed baseline. All results are self-reported, single-paper and trained from scratch with R50, not fine-tuned from DINOv2. A claim that DQ-DETR's gain was concentrated in APvt was refuted (1-2). Cost: these are architecture changes (density maps, dynamic queries, high-res pyramids), so likely several days to port into RF-DETR. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03507, https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05741v2, https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00825
- **Scaling query count to object density is a component worth roughly +2 AP on dense tiny-object scenes. This is the most transferable DETR idea for depots with a median of 76 boxes per image.** (low, 3-0 (claim 12)). Dome-DETR ablation on a D-FINE-S baseline (AI-TOD-V2 test): 30.1 AP becomes 33.3 with all three components (APvt 14.0 to 17.8); DeFE+PAQI alone gives +2.0 AP. The paper's text mislabels the DeFE+MS-WAS row as 'DeFE alone'. Simply raising num_queries/num_select in RF-DETR is a simplification of PAQI, not something the paper tested. It is cheap (config change, under 1 h), but higher query counts may need longer fine-tuning. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05741v2
- **Copy-paste augmentation gives small, setting-dependent gains. It helps most in low-data regimes combined with large scale jitter, gives about 0 or slightly negative results when placement is naive, and needs the original images kept to avoid overfitting to pasting artifacts.** (high, 3-0 (merged claims 14,16,17,18,22,23)). Simple Copy-Paste: about +10 box AP at 10% of COCO, but only +4.8 of that comes from copy-paste on top of LSJ. In full-data training: +1.3 box AP averaged over 7 models. Kisantal 2019 (COCO small): oversampling + copy-paste takes det AP_S from 0.167 to 0.179 (+7.1% relative); replacing the originals with augmented images gives 0.161 (worse). VisDrone 2026: Cut-Paste-Learn takes RT-DETR from 58.9 to 58.7 AP50 and YOLOv5 from 56.7 to 56.2. The full scene-aware IP-CC pipeline adds only +1.5 (RT-DETR) to +2.1 AP50 and costs 2x the images and about 4x the training time. Copy-paste for boxes only (no masks) on 443 images has not been tested. Cost: 2-4 h for a context-aware paste (e.g. onto road/lot pixels near other trucks). Risk: domain artifacts. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.07177, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.07296, https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-18-00647/article_deploy/remotesensing-18-00647.pdf
- **Class-balanced repeat-factor sampling is the cheapest plug-in for rare classes and adds to copy-paste in single-stage training. Instance-aware RFS (IRFS) is a better variant that changes only the sampler.** (medium, 3-0 (merged claims 15,19,20,21)). LVIS single-stage: RFS + Copy-Paste gives +2.4 AP and +8.7 APr. RFS hurts in two-stage training with class-rebalanced losses. IRFS uses r_c = max(1, sqrt(t / sqrt(f_img,c * f_box,c))) and takes the max over classes per image. It beats RFS by +5.0 APr (+40%) and +1.5 mAP with R-101, and common and frequent classes also improve. Oversampling small-object images 3x gave about +1 AP_S but lowered AP_L. With only 5 classes the long-tail effect will be much smaller than on LVIS (1203 classes). Cost: under 1 h in any DETR data loader. Low risk, but do not stack it with a class-rebalanced loss. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.07177, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.08069, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.07296

Caveats:
1. **Coverage gaps.** No claims survived verification for several areas in the question: adding P2/P3 levels to the RF-DETR projector, SAHI-style slicing and overlap, multi-scale TTA, WBF, upsampling factor, focal/varifocal variants, DotD, DNTR, denoising-query tweaks, and fine-grained second-stage crop classifiers. The ranking of those levers rests on earlier use-case research (tiling +10-18 AP50, 2-4x upscaling, about 25% overlap), not on this run.

2. **Transfer gap.** Nearly every gain was measured on anchor-based CNNs (Faster R-CNN, DetectoRS, Mask R-CNN) or on DETRs trained from scratch with R50. None was measured when fine-tuning a DINOv2-backbone RF-DETR on 443 images. Most numbers are COCO-style AP rather than AP50; AP50 gains are often smaller (Dome-DETR adds only +0.6 AP50 over DQ-DETR).

3. **Source quality.** All results are self-reported by the authors, single-run, with no variance reported. Several are arXiv preprints (Dome-DETR, HEDS-DETR, DQ-DETR) or a workshop paper (IRFS). Gains of 1 AP or less are plausibly within seed noise.

4. **Benchmark mismatch.** LVIS long-tail results overstate what a 5-class problem will see, and VisDrone is low-altitude drone imagery, not satellite imagery.

5. **Ranking is our judgment.** The gain-per-hour ranking combines the reported numbers with our own cost estimates. None of the sources ranks these levers.

## Deep research 3 (11 Oct): public truck-class datasets/checkpoints (verified, 3-vote)

I found no public checkpoint that can serve as a drop-in ensemble member for xView's 5 truck classes. The only dataset with matching truck classes is FAIR1M (0.3-0.8 m GSD, oriented boxes). It has Cargo Truck and Truck Tractor but no Box, Flatbed or Liquid classes. FAIR1M models also do badly on Truck Tractor themselves: under 1 AP50 on v1.0, and 5-36 on v2.0 depending on the method. Cargo Truck is the only class that transfers well (about 43-56 AP50 in-domain), so a FAIR1M model mostly helps Cargo and does little for Tractor. The most practical FAIR1M-trained checkpoint is MTP's (FAIR1M-2.0, oriented boxes). It runs on old MMRotate, downloads only from Baidu or OneDrive, and its backbones are heavy, so it is a long shot in a 2-day budget on a T4. Two directly downloadable xView detectors exist on GitHub: SAHI's TOOD, FCOS and VFNet. TOOD reaches only 20.6 AP50 averaged over all 60 xView classes, and no per-class numbers are published, so they are a cheap but uncertain second ensemble member. DOTA, DIOR, the RVSA checkpoints and GOOD have only coarse vehicle classes or no released weights. COFGA has the best-matching labels (prime mover, tanker, flatbed, enclosed box) but is aerial imagery at 5-15 cm, with attribute tags rather than detection classes. Ranked by expected gain on the weak classes per hour of effort: (1) SAHI xView TOOD as an ensemble member, (2) MTP's FAIR1M checkpoint mapped only to Cargo Truck, (3) everything else, which doesn't fit a 2-day budget.


- **FAIR1M is the only public very-high-resolution satellite dataset found with fine-grained truck classes. Its vehicle classes are Small Car, Bus, Cargo Truck, Dump Truck, Van, Trailer, Tractor, Truck Tractor, Excavator and other-vehicle. Imagery is 0.3-0.8 m GSD from Gaofen and Google Earth, with 15k+ images, about 1M instances, oriented boxes and 37 sub-categories (34 if the 'Other' classes are excluded). v1.0 and v2.0 share 16,488 training images, and test labels are hidden behind an evaluation server.** (high, 3-0 (merged claims 5, 11, 12, 14, 15, 19)). FAIR1M paper section 3.1 and abstract; torchgeo class ids 9-18; arXiv 2512.24074 section 4.1. All three-way votes were unanimous. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.05569, https://torchgeo.readthedocs.io/en/v0.2.1/_modules/torchgeo/datasets/fair1m.html, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.10515, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.24074
- **FAIR1M labels only partly match xView. Cargo Truck maps directly. Truck Tractor maps partly, because FAIR1M boxes may include the attached trailer. FAIR1M has no Box, Flatbed or Liquid truck classes. Its Tractor class is an agricultural tractor and must not be mapped to xView Truck Tractor. Van, Excavator and Dump Truck are outside the 5 target classes.** (high, 3-0). Class lists, plus verifier notes on label mismatch. FAIR1M's 0.8 m upper end is about 2.7x coarser than xView's, so a truck is about 3 px wide there versus about 8 px in xView. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.05569, https://torchgeo.readthedocs.io/en/v0.2.1/_modules/torchgeo/datasets/fair1m.html
- **FAIR1M-trained detectors handle Cargo Truck reasonably well, at about 43-56 AP50: ROI Transformer 54.0 in the original paper, PETDet 55.6 on v2.0, 55.3 for the best 2025 method, and 33-43 for ResNet-50 models on v1.0. Truck Tractor is unreliable: 0.24-0.72 on v1.0 across eight ResNet-50 detectors, and 5.4-36.3 on v2.0 depending on method (ORCNN 5.39, FRCNN 5.93, SFRNet 29.35, PETDet 33.14, best 36.33). Trailer stays at 11-16 and agricultural Tractor at 1-16. A FAIR1M ensemble member would therefore help xView Cargo (currently 0.14) far more than Truck Tractor (0.03).** (high, 3-0 (merged claims 3, 4, 6, 13, 20)). Per-class AP tables: 2407.17738 Table I; PETDet Table II; 2512.24074 Table 6 (baselines from the DRNet paper); FAIR1M Table 2. All figures are oriented-box AP and are not directly comparable to xView axis-aligned mAP50. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.17738, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.10515, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.24074, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.05569
- **MTP (ViTAE-Transformer/MTP) releases downloadable task-specific detectors. These include FAIR1M-2.0 Oriented R-CNN models (ViT-B+RVSA 51.92 mAP, ViT-L+RVSA 53.00, InternImage-XL 50.93) and xView RetinaNet models with horizontal boxes (16.4 / 19.4 / 18.2 AP50 over all xView classes). ## xView paper read + citation crawl (11 Oct; agent read 1802.07856, its references, ~15 of 456 citing papers via Semantic Scholar)
- **Label protocol (Lam et al. 2018):** labellers assign a child class only "if the labeler can confidently determine" it, else the parent (e.g. generic Truck); QC batches accepted at precision 0.75 / recall 0.95 vs gold @IoU 0.5 (i.e. up to ~25% imprecision tolerated); clusters sometimes labelled as one object. Shermeyer & Van Etten (1812.04098): "many objects are mislabeled or simply missed"; Liquid (149 ex.) "looks very similar to" Box (3653 ex.).
- **Class definitions (xView Table 1 names):** Box / Flatbed / Liquid are "Truck Tractor w/ Box Trailer / w/ Flatbed Trailer / w/ Liquid Tank" — i.e. tractor+trailer combinations; plain "Truck Tractor" is the tractor alone. The tractor cab is shared by four of the five classes -> a mechanism for Truck Tractor being the weakest class in every run (RFX0 0.030).
- **Per-class SSD AP50 (Lam et al. Table 1, vanilla/multires/aug):** Cargo 0.049/0.097/0.045; Box 0.136/0.086/0.119; Flatbed 0.032/0.126/0.034; Tractor 0.005/0.213/0.000; Liquid 0.018/0.474/0.000. Zand et al. 2021 (2104.11854, Table IV, best): Cargo 0.135, Tractor 0.244, Flatbed 0.178 (Box/Liquid not reported). RFX0 zero-shot Box 0.281 / Flatbed 0.327 already exceed every published per-class number found (different splits — caveat).
- **Usable ideas from citing papers:** (1) WBF ensembles of heterogeneous pipelines: 29.88 vs 20.87 xView val mAP for 5 pipelines vs 1 (1810.10110, Table III); WBF code 1910.13302. (2) mild zoom TTA (~1.1-1.25x), not 2x (1810.10110 qualitative). (3) parent/sibling labels as ignore regions (from the protocol; needs xView geojson; no evidence of size). (4) copy-paste of rare classes (Kisantal 1902.07296, +7.1% rel. COCO small). (5) co-occurrence context (1810.10110 Fig. 10: Flatbed with container lots/cranes; Box with buildings/vehicle lots) — our context smoothing is an instance of this.

## Deep-research synthesis (12 Oct 2026; 103 agents, 21 sources, 25 claims verified, 20 confirmed / 5 refuted)
Scoped to 4 fronts for the RFX track. Full machine output archived; headline findings below (confidence + vote as verified).

### Front 4 — realistic ceiling: 0.75 is above the literature ceiling (HIGH, 3-0)
- PETDet (ResNet50), SOTA-class fine-grained aerial: FAIR1M-v1.0 **42.96 AP**, v2.0 **48.81 AP**, MAR20 85.9, ShipRSImageNet 74.9 (arXiv:2312.10515; github canoe-Z/PETDet). 37-class taxonomy, not a direct 5-truck analogue, but shows large fine-grained overhead taxonomies cap well under 0.75.
- xView origin (Lam et al. 2018, same 0.3 m WV-3 + exact truck taxonomy): best SSD baseline **0.259 mAP over 60 classes**, trucks/vans lower (arXiv:1802.07856).
- Converges with our own perfect-box oracle **0.43**. Refuted in verification (do NOT cite): ROI-Transformer 19.12% mAP_F, "~34-41 AP50 ceiling", MTP 53.0 FAIR1M-as-ceiling.
- **Evidence-based expectation: ~0.35-0.55 mAP50 is defensible from 0.20; 0.75 has NO cited precedent for a 5-way fine-grained overhead truck task without overlap.** A near-0.75 number on these classes should be scrutinised for train/val overlap. [strengthens the honesty framing: target is above the field ceiling]

### Front 2 — the bottleneck is DISCRIMINATION, and the matched lever is two-stage detect->crop->classify (HIGH, 3-0)
- Lit confirms fine-grained overhead difficulty is semantic confusion (inter-class similarity / intra-class variation), not localization (FGOD survey arXiv:2407.17738; CoFGA arXiv:2105.12786) — matches our oracle 0.43.
- **Bou et al. CVPRW 2024 (EarthVision):** replace the detector's class head with a PROTOTYPE classifier = averaged box-crop **DINOv2 ViT-L/14** embeddings, cosine match. SIMD novel classes 5/10/30-shot **35.4/39.0/41.2 mAP50** vs DE-ViT 20.4/20.4/20.1 and supervised YOLOv5 16.6/19.6/29.5; "visual features largely superior to vision-language models". Code: github.com/xavibou/ovdsat. CAVEAT: SIMD = large aircraft at coarser GSD; their RPN bottlenecks on novel geometry (OPPOSITE of our solved-localization regime -> favourable for us). Validates DIRECTION, not a 0.75 number.
- Corroborating: RT-DETR + fine-tuned ViT-B/16 two-stage -> 0.94 acc on 6-class vehicle body-type (arXiv:2606.05149).

### Front 2 — rare-class levers (Tractor/Liquid near AP-0)
- **Seesaw Loss** (CVPR 2021, arXiv:2008.10032): rebalances per-class pos/neg gradients; beats EQL +3.9/+1.4 AP on LVIS. Targets exactly the collapse mode (head negatives swamp tail). CAVEAT: LVIS softmax-CE, not DETR set-prediction -> transfer plausible, unproven; don't cite LVIS deltas as expected gains.
- **IRFS** instance-aware repeat-factor sampling (MEDIUM, arXiv:2305.08069): fixes RFS's image-only frequency flaw.
- **ColMix** collage copy-paste (HIGH, arXiv:2305.13509): **xView-39 mAP 11.2 -> 15.0-15.3 (+34-37% rel)**; collage 14.1 > Mosaic 13.1 > baseline 11.2 > plain copy-paste 10.8. Use the RELATIVE lift (COCO-style mAP, not mAP50). Counter-lit: Dvornik ECCV2018 (context matters for detection).

### Front 1 — external init: helps backbone/localization, NOT discrimination
- **RVSA / MTP** (ViT-B/L+RVSA, InternImage-XL; **MIT**; OneDrive ckpts): DOTA-v1.0 **81 mAP** OBB (arXiv:2208.03987; github ViTAE-Transformer/{Remote-Sensing-RVSA,MTP}). DOTA has only generic small/large-vehicle -> NO truck subtypes. 81 mAP is generic 15-class, not a 5-truck ceiling.
- **SatlasPretrain** (Swin-v2 B/T, RN50/152; **ODC-BY**; pip auto-download from HF allenai/satlas-pretrain): 0.5-2 m NAIP (coarser than our 0.3 m), random heads, 137 infra/maritime cats, no truck subtypes (github allenai/satlaspretrain_models).
- **FAIR1M** (0.3-0.8 m, ISPRS, arXiv:2103.05569): most resolution-compatible source WITH a fine-grained Truck subcategory. FLAG: the claim its subtypes map cleanly to xView's 5 (cargo/tractor/trailer/van) was REFUTED (1-2) -> inspect taxonomy manually before using as supervised pretrain.

### Front 3 — DETR anti-forgetting: thinly evidenced (LOW)
- No quantified DETR-specific freeze/LoRA/L2-SP/EWC recipe survived verification. RF-DETR LoRA "untested" per maintainers (Nov 2025, gh issue 469). DINO ViT-B/16 loses >70% ImageNet acc after 10 fine-tune iters (quantifies our collapse). -> **Strongest available anti-forgetting answer = the two-stage frozen-backbone prototype head, which SIDESTEPS head fine-tuning entirely.**

### Decision implication
The single highest-EV next experiment is **two-stage: class-agnostic boxes (RFX0 / a detector) -> crop -> frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 prototype classifier (cosine)**. It attacks the CONFIRMED discrimination bottleneck, needs NO fine-tuning (no forgetting), is cheap (frozen features), and our regime (localization already decent) is favourable vs the one caveat in the cited validation. Supersedes further RFX fine-tune variants in expected value. Open: does the 0.43 oracle cap lift once a DINOv2 prototype head replaces the detector head?
